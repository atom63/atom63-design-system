---
title: Designing for interruptions
description: What eleven clinic front desks taught me about screens that survive being left half-done.
date: '2026-02-08'
---

Most interfaces assume the person using them is paying attention. At a clinic front desk, nobody is: the phone rings, someone arrives, a nurse leans over the counter. I timed it once. A receptionist was interrupted every 90 seconds.

## Three rules I now start from

1. **Nothing is lost when someone looks away.** A half-filled form is a draft, not an error.
2. **The next action is always obvious.** After an interruption, the screen says where you were.
3. **No modal owns the screen.** A dialog that blocks the desk turns a phone call into a queue.

<Callout type="success">
  None of this is specific to clinics. Support tools, dispatch consoles and kitchens all work this way.
</Callout>

> The best compliment I got was "I forgot it was new".

## What it changed

Booking time halved, but the number I care about more is the one we stopped hearing: "where was I?".
